Strait of Hormuz: Strategic Geopolitics & Architecture

The Strait of Hormuz Bottleneck
Separating Iran from Oman and the UAE, the strait is only 21 miles wide at its narrowest point, with the commercial shipping lane comprising two 2-mile-wide channels separated by a 2-mile buffer zone. It represents the single most critical transit lane for global crude oil and liquefied natural gas (LNG).
Bypass Pipelines: Mitigating Chokepoint Vulnerability
Key overland routes circumventing Hormuz include the ADCOP (Abu Dhabi Crude Oil Pipeline) running 360 km from Habshan to the Port of Fujairah on the Gulf of Oman (~1.5M bpd capacity), and Iran's Goreh-Jask pipeline (~1,000 km, ~300k–1M bpd target capacity) exiting outside the Persian Gulf.
Major Upstream Centers Feeding the Chokepoint
Over 80% of crude passing Hormuz originates from Saudi Arabia, Iraq, the UAE, Kuwait, and Iran, heading primarily toward Asian destinations (China, India, Japan, South Korea).
Fujairah Bunkering & Storage Hub
Located immediately south of the Strait on the Gulf of Oman, Fujairah ranks among the world's premier bunkering and independent oil storage terminals, allowing vessel loading without entering the congested Persian Gulf waters.

Dubai / Oman Marker & Pricing Structure

The Middle East benchmark complex is anchored by Dubai and Oman crudes (~31° API, medium sour), which serve as the pricing reference for billions of barrels of Persian Gulf crude sold on long-term contracts to Asia-Pacific refiners.

Data Quality & Clean Filtering

To eliminate mapping artifacts and ensure clean executive metrics, all spatial features flagged as Unnamed, null, undefined, or blank in concession and well attributes are strictly excluded from calculations and visualizations.
